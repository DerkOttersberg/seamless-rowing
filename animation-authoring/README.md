# Original fixed-seat poses

Open `rowing.bbmodel` in Blockbench's free-model editor. The original torso clip contains eight Catmull–Rom keys and one repeated loop endpoint over 0.8 seconds. The neutral authoring mesh shows the seated torso, head and two six-pixel arm segments. It is reference geometry, not a replacement Minecraft model or bundled player texture.

`common/src/main/resources/assets/seamlessrowing/animations/fixed_seat.json` is the runtime export in radians. `verifyAnimationAuthoring` compares every exported torso key with this source. Resource reload validates the clip and atomically replaces its snapshot. Invalid resource-pack clips fall back to these original poses.

The runtime retimes this curve to each native paddle's synchronized phase. Preview.3 increases the original pitch keys to approximately −11.5° through +12.6° and distributes each bend through the spine: the lumbar angle is 55% of the clip, growing to 145% at the chest. The hip center stays fixed and the legs retain their native seated pose. Turning adds up to about five degrees of shoulder twist. The head and arm roots follow the chest; item use and attacks yield the torso to their ordinary pose.

Arms are solved from the rendered native paddle handle rather than replaying canned hand positions. Upper arm and forearm lengths remain six model pixels. Preview.3 replaces the two independent capped blocks with a continuous textured envelope, sampled at half-pixel intervals. A tangent-continuous quadratic bend rounds the elbow between the rigid proximal arm and distal forearm, with a gentle inner-fold taper. Shared cross-sections have no internal caps or gaps; the wrist stays at the original lower-bone tip. Skin, sleeves and supported armor use their original UVs, including the native trim/glint pipeline. First person omits proximal shoulder geometry near the camera without moving the elbow or grip.

The native torso part and renderer are retained. Half-pixel texture bands follow the curved spine and supported vanilla chest armor uses the same deformation. Ordinary body geometry is restored before the next non-rowing pose. Unsupported body topology disables rowing visuals rather than separating the head from a custom torso. Render snapshots contain immutable bone rotations and spine parameters; mesh deformation reads those parameters during deferred submission and never advances the animation clock.

The spine uses each paddle track's six-tick startup and four-tick recovery, retaining its current lean when vanilla resets the stopped paddle phase. Extended reaches add at most 1.5 pixels of smooth scapular protraction toward the handle, keeping both arm bones fixed and avoiding a locked elbow. Only visible skin/sleeve surfaces deform for the selected perspective. Intentionally hidden or relocated heads are preserved, including FirstPersonModel's distant-head hiding technique. Native probes compare the submitted wrist surface with the bone marker, verify every shared mesh ring, measure visible skin-neck attachment and verify restoration of the original body mesh. Expanded armor geometry is measured separately from the skin neck.

Preview.2 uses a posterior elbow pole in native model coordinates (+Y down, +Z behind the player), with modest lateral clearance. This lowers the elbow on forward reaches while keeping its hinge orientation consistent on rearward reaches; an explicit downward bias could invert that orientation. Both segments share an anatomical hinge frame: the forearm flexes forward about the upper arm's local X axis instead of independently acquiring an arbitrary roll. Small shaft-driven pronation is bounded and fades continuously as the shaft approaches the forearm axis. Hand contact, limb lengths and tick interpolation are preserved; skin and equipment use the same solver.

Technique was informed by the drive/recovery sequence in [World Rowing's basic technique reference](https://worldrowing.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/Level2Chapter3BasicRowingTechnique_English.pdf). Minecraft's fixed seat and short single-bladed paddles require restrained shoulder and torso motion; sliding-seat motion would disconnect the character from the boat. No external motion-capture clip or third-party animation asset is redistributed.
